7a. The Fork Ledger, credits and creation records

  • What it is. The Fork Ledger is an append-only public record of signed entries (publications, credits, agreements, creation milestones). Fork validates and orders the entries, copies of the daily batches are kept by devices on the Fork network, and each day a fingerprint of the record is sealed on a public blockchain (Solana; devnet during the beta).
  • Your account key. The ledger needs a key that belongs to you. It is created only when you first do something that writes to the ledger, never just for using the apps. You choose how to back up its 12 recovery words: on paper (recommended) or in a password manager. If you lose both the key and the backup, Fork cannot recover it for you.
  • What a record proves, and what it doesn't. A record shows that an entry existed no later than the sealed day. It does not prove authorship, that a date you declared is true, or that a work is original.
  • Credits. A credit is Unconfirmed until the person credited confirms it with their own key. They can dispute or withdraw it. People you invite by email register a normal Fork account to confirm.
  • Disputes. Fork never decides who is right. While a credit is disputed, the track stays on sale but nobody is paid from it. You have 180 days to take the dispute to a court or collecting society; without that, the dispute lapses and sales are paid by the original split.
  • Agreements with a label. The text of an agreement never leaves the parties' devices. By default only the parties see the percentages; the public sees only that a signed agreement exists. Any party may publish the summary.
  • Creation milestones. Showing dates and counts (never file names) of a track's creation milestones on its public page is on by default. You can turn it off for each track.
  • Public and permanent. Entries are public, permanent and cannot be edited or deleted. Names, emails, file names, prices and splits in clear are never written to them. See the Privacy Notice for how erasure works.
  • Exports. DDEX files you import enter as Unconfirmed credits. Imported credits are never treated as confirmed until each person signs.
